# R3: Radiatively coupled liquid U–Pu, helium-cooled Mo tubes, direct Brayton

Variant of [R1](R1.md) in which the cooled panels are banks of molybdenum tubes carrying helium at 7 MPa straight to a Brayton turbine. Fuel tubes, gap and core layout are those of R1. There is no liquid coolant, no coolant-void reactivity and no intermediate exchanger. The cost is a ~12% lower flux, from the helium film, and a pressurised tube at ~1,500 °C facing vacuum. Verdict: the simplest plant if Mo or W tubes can carry the pressure. Creep is the open issue.

Numbers: *(calc)* from [analysis/new-geometries/](../../analysis/new-geometries/README.md), *(db)* from the property database, *(approx.)* otherwise.

| Item | Value |

| Coolant tubes | Mo (TZM) or W, 4 mm bore, 1 mm wall, coated for emissivity on the gap side; He 7 MPa, 1,000 → 1,500 °C, ~385 kg/s *(calc)* |
| Gap | Vacuum; a coolant-tube leak pressurises it with He (detectable, and it raises coupling) |
| Core *(calc)* | ~17 m³ and ~117 t fuel (R1 scaled by the 12% lower flux) |

## Radiative heat-transfer sizing

Helium-side resistance (h ~4 kW/m²K plus 1 mm Mo) is ~2.6 × 10⁻⁴ m²K/W, six times the aluminium panel's. At a coated 2,000 °C tube and 1,500 °C outlet the average flux is 0.67 MW/m², against 0.77 for R1. At a 1,300 °C outlet it is 0.74, and at 1,700 °C 0.57 *(calc)*. A bare Mo receiver (ε ~0.25) would cut the flux by about a factor of three, as for R2.

Hoop stress in the coolant tubes is p·r/t = 14 MPa *(calc)*. That is a modest stress for TZM at 1,300 °C but marginal at 1,500 °C over years *(approx.)*. W tubes are stronger at temperature but absorb more.

## Neutronics

Infinite medium, 2,100 K *(calc, neutronics.py)*:

| Quantity | R3 | R1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pu/(U+Pu) for k∞ = 1.15 | 9.8% | 10.2% |
| Conversion ratio at k∞ = 1.15 | 1.26 | 1.32 |
| Median flux energy | ~215 keV | ~196 keV |

With no aluminium the spectrum is harder, so slightly less Pu is needed. The Mo tubes absorb more than AlN, so conversion is a little lower. Thinner or isotope-tailored tubes would close the gap.

Helium has no void worth. The only coolant-related reactivity event is gap pressurisation by a tube leak, which is negligible neutronically.

## Power cycle

Direct helium Brayton at a 1,300–1,500 °C turbine inlet. The ceiling is blade materials (~1,400–1,500 °C, approx.), not the reactor. Recuperated, the cycle can return helium near 1,000 °C. That inlet is fine because the fuel never touches the coolant tubes, so there is no freezing limit on the inlet.

## Go / no-go tests

1. As R1 tests 1, 2 and 4.
2. Creep-rupture of coated TZM and W tubes at 7 MPa internal pressure, 1,400–1,600 °C, in vacuum, for ≥ 1,000 h.
3. Helium impurity control against Mo at 1,500 °C.
