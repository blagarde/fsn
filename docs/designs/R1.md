# R1: Radiatively coupled liquid U–Pu, aluminium-cooled AlN panels

Reference design of the radiatively coupled family (geometry L7, heat-transport mode K-R in the [geometry study](../studies/geometry-by-phase.md)). Liquid U–Pu fuel sits in rows of vertical tungsten tubes. The tubes radiate across a 2 mm nitrogen-filled gap to AlN panels with aluminium flowing inside. **No material touches both fuel and coolant.** That removes the [A2](A2.md) critical risk, a single wall wetted by liquid uranium on one face and liquid aluminium on the other. The price is surface and inventory. At a 2,000 °C tube surface with a coated (emissivity ~0.8) tube, the core needs ~1,400 m² of tube surface, ~15 m³ of core and ~100 t of fuel for 1,000 MWth. A2 needs ~3.3 m³ and 35 t. The core still breeds well. Clean-critical Pu fraction is ~8.9% and the breeding ratio is ~1.7 with a 40 cm blanket (homogenised Monte Carlo, upper-bound proxy). Aluminium leaves at 1,500 °C, and 1,650 °C would cost only ~10% more area. Verdict: physically plausible. Feasibility hangs on one surface property, emissivity at 2,000 °C, and on the W–U container (also open for A2).

All numbers are labelled: *(calc)* from the scripts in [analysis/new-geometries/](../../analysis/new-geometries/README.md), *(db)* from the property database, *(approx.)* otherwise. Sizing details and the comparison with other geometries are in the [scoping study](../studies/new-geometries-scoping.md#l7-radiatively-coupled-pool).

## Configuration

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Thermal power | 1,000 MWth |
| Fuel tubes | W, 20 mm bore, 1 mm wall, 23 mm pitch in rows; outer surface textured or coated for emissivity ~0.8 *(approx.)* |
| Gap | 2 mm each side of a tube row, N2 at ~1 bar (stabilises the AlN, adds a little conduction, carries leak signals) |
| Cooled panels | 1 mm AlN (N-15) walls, 4 mm aluminium channel; one panel between every two tube rows; row pitch 32 mm |
| Volume fractions *(calc, homogenised cell)* | Fuel 42.7% · W 9.0% · AlN 6.3% · Al 12.5% · gap 29.6% |
| Temperatures | Tube surface 2,000 °C (design limit); fuel peak ~2,120 °C by conduction alone; Al 1,000 → 1,500 °C, inlet at the top |
| Core *(calc)* | ~15 m³, D ≈ H ≈ 2.7 m; ~1,400 m² of tube surface; 66 MW/m³ average |
| Fuel inventory *(calc)* | ~103 t liquid U–Pu; ~9–10 t Pu at 8.9–10% Pu/(U+Pu) |
| Aluminium flow *(calc)* | ~1,700 kg/s for 500 K rise (JANAF Cp) |
| Fuel management | Tubes hang from a top plenum holding the free surface, vent and fill/drain; online processing as in A2 |

The tubes form tiers ≤ ~1 m tall, each fed from its own plenum. The reason is the hydrostatic head of liquid U–Pu (16 t/m³). A 1 m column puts 0.16 MPa on the can, giving a hoop stress of 1.6 MPa in a 1 mm wall at r = 10 mm. A 2.5 m column would give 3.9 MPa, above tungsten's few-MPa creep strength at 2,000 °C. Flat fuel plates would be stiffer per unit area but bend: a 20 mm span carries 16 MPa at a 0.5 m head *(calc)*. So the reference uses tubes.

## Radiative heat-transfer sizing

Net flux between grey surfaces: q = σ(T_h⁴ − T_c⁴)/(1/ε₁ + 1/ε₂ − 1). For a tube row facing panels, the factor becomes 1/ε₁ + (A_tube/A_panel)(1/ε₂ − 1). The aluminium heats along the panel, so the average flux is the harmonic mean over coolant temperature. With the inlet at the top, the liquid fuel cooled at the top sinks and stirs each tube. The tube surface is therefore taken as uniform. For unzoned solid fuel (S9) this would not hold.

| Case (Al 1,000 °C in) | Avg flux on panel (MW/m²) | Tube surface (m²) | Core (m³) | Fuel (t) | Fuel peak (°C) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Reference: coated W 0.8, 2,000 °C, Al out 1,500 °C** | 0.77 | 1,413 | 15.1 | 103 | 2,122 |
| Al out 1,300 °C | 0.83 | ~1,300 | ~14 | ~95 | ~2,120 |
| Al out 1,700 °C | 0.67 | ~1,630 | ~17 | ~118 | ~2,120 |
| Bare W (ε 0.3), 2,000 °C | 0.33 | 3,177 | 33.8 | 231 | 2,054 |
| Coated W, 2,200 °C (stretch) | 1.16 | 931 | 9.9 | 68 | 2,376 |
| Smaller tubes, r = 7.5 mm | 0.77 | 1,409 | 12.8 | 75 | 2,096 |

All *(calc, thermal.py; emissivities approx.)*. A2 for comparison: ~3 MW/m² across 333 m² of wall.

- **Emissivity is the strongest lever.** Bare tungsten (~0.3) more than doubles everything. The receiver side matters too. AlN at ~0.8 *(approx.)* is a good absorber. A bare molybdenum receiver (~0.25) would cut the flux to 0.27 MW/m² *(calc)*.
- **Outlet temperature is cheap.** Because radiation goes as T⁴, raising the aluminium outlet from 1,300 to 1,700 °C costs only ~25% more area. A2's outlet is capped by the conduction drop through the fuel and wall; R1's is capped by the receiver chemistry (below).
- **Gap gas.** At 1 bar between 2,000 °C and 1,400 °C surfaces, N2 adds ~0.035 MW/m² by conduction and He ~0.18 MW/m² *(calc)*. Radiation is 0.71 MW/m² for coated tubes and 0.30 for bare tubes at those temperatures. Convection is negligible (Ra ~0.002). Changing the gap gas from N2 to He raises coupling by ~25%: a slow power or temperature trim that no wall has to survive.

### Cross-check with the core-thermal model (2026-11-21)

The [core-thermal program](../studies/core-thermal.md) (`analysis/core-thermal/core.py`, design mode at 1,000 MWth, Al 1,000→1,500 °C, ε 0.8/0.8, fuel limit 2,100 °C, liquid fuel strongly mixed) reproduces the sizing above **only if the power is flattened radially**. The ~1,400 m² assumes every tube radiates at the full flux. In a core with the natural (unzoned) power shape, the hottest tube near the centre reaches the limit first, so the core-average flux falls to ~0.33 MW/m² *(calc)*:

| Case | Tube surface | Core | Fuel | Fissile Pu |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unzoned (radial peaking 2.0, axial cosine) | 3,070 m² | 36 m³ | 245 t | 15 t |
| Radially zoned (peaking 1.0), as on this page | 1,540 m² | 18 m³ | 123 t | 7.8 t |
| Zoned, ε 0.9/0.9 or fuel limit 2,200 °C | ~1,260 m² | ~15 m³ | ~100 t | 6.5 t |
| Zoned, tube bore radius 5 mm (wall 1 mm) | 1,450 m² | 8.4 m³ | 58 t | 3.9 t |
| Zoned, bore radius 3 mm, fuel fraction 0.35 | 1,440 m² | 6.2 m³ | 35 t | 2.7 t |
| Zoned, bore 5 mm, ε 0.9, fuel limit 2,200 °C | 975 m² | 5.7 m³ | 39 t | 2.7 t |
| Unzoned, bare W (ε 0.3) | 7,450 m² | 87 m³ | 596 t | 36 t |

Two conclusions follow:
- **The surface is set by radiation physics and cannot shrink much.** It falls only with higher emissivity, a hotter fuel surface (flux ∝ T⁴) or flatter power. Radial power zoning (Pu-fraction zones) is a requirement, not a refinement: without it the core doubles.
- **The inventory is set by tube bore, not by the surface.** Fuel volume per m² of surface is about r/2. Going from a 10 mm to a 5 mm bore radius halves the fuel at the same surface. The minimum is set by criticality (more Pu fraction as the fuel fraction drops) and by the number of tubes, about 4× more at a 5 mm bore. The 20 mm bore was chosen for the hydrostatic and handling arguments above. Smaller tubes trade them for inventory.

## Neutronics

Infinite homogenised medium (`screen()`, ENDF/B-VIII.0, 2,100 K), reactor-grade Pu (approx. vector), depleted U, N-15 99.9% in AlN *(calc, neutronics.py and l7_detail.py)*:

| Quantity | R1 | A2 reference (67% fuel) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pu/(U+Pu) for k∞ = 1.15 | 10.2% | 9.1% |
| Conversion ratio at k∞ = 1.15 (core only) | 1.32 | 1.44 |
| Median flux energy | ~196 keV | ~196 keV |
| Coolant (Al) fully voided | +3,000 pcm | +3,700 pcm |
| Finite core + 40 cm blanket: clean-critical Pu, breeding ratio | 8.9%, 1.71 (15.1 m³) | 8.1%, 1.90 (3.3 m³) |

Higher-statistics check of the reference (50,000 particles × 100 batches, 30 inactive; *calc, l7_detail.py*): at 10.18% Pu with the N2-filled gap, k∞ = 1.1493 ± 0.0003 and conversion ratio 1.326. Worths relative to that state:

| Event | Worth (pcm) | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gap loses its N2 (evacuated) | −27 ± 37 | Negligible: the gap gas is a thermal and chemical choice, not a neutronic one |
| Aluminium voided from the panels | +3,010 ± 35 | Positive in the infinite medium (spectrum hardens); needs a breach, since Al boils ~1,000 K above the outlet |
| Gap flooded with aluminium (panel breach) | −6,260 ± 36 | Strongly negative: Al in the gap dilutes and softens |
| Gap flooded with lead (R2 panel breach, otherwise R1) | −2,950 ± 33 | Negative |

A panel breach that floods the gap shuts the core down rather than driving it up. Only loss of coolant from the panels without flooding the gap adds reactivity.

- **It breeds, a little less than A2.** The 43% fuel fraction (A2 67%) costs ~1 point of Pu and ~0.1 of conversion ratio. The larger core leaks less, which recovers part of it. Both breeding ratios are upper-bound proxies: homogenised, clean (no fission products), a thick blanket and no control margin.
- **Doubling time is ~4× A2's** for the same breeding ratio, because the inventory is ~3.5× larger. Proxy, with fissile 69% of Pu, +20% out of core and 1.25 kg/day consumption *(approx.)*: R1 ~23 years vs A2 ~6 years at the breeding ratios above.
- **Positive coolant-void worth** in the infinite medium. Removing aluminium hardens the spectrum. In a finite core leakage offsets part of it, and the coolant here is only 12.5% of the volume. Al boils at 2,518 °C *(db)*, ~1,000 K above the outlet, so voiding needs a breach, not boiling. The finite-core worth is open.
- **Spectrum class F, not H.** The median flux energy (~196 keV) sits just below the 200 keV boundary of axis 4, for A2 too. The A2 designation (`H`) may need revisiting.

## Power cycle

Aluminium at 1,500 °C goes to an Al-to-helium exchanger with AlN or ZrB2 walls, as for A2, then to a helium Brayton or combined cycle. Turbine inlet is limited to ~1,400–1,500 °C by blade materials *(approx.)*. Process heat up to ~1,450 °C fits the [industrial-heat](../studies/industrial-heat.md) cases. Coolant inlet is free: A2 needs ≥ 1,200 °C to keep its ~1,100 °C fuel from freezing on the tube, while in R1 the fuel never touches a cold surface. So a 1,000 °C inlet works and a deeper cycle ΔT halves the Al flow compared with A2.

## Go / no-go tests

1. **Emissivity at temperature:** total hemispherical emissivity of textured W and ZrC-coated W at 1,800–2,200 °C for ≥ 1,000 h in N2 and vacuum. Below ~0.6 the design roughly doubles in size.
2. **Container capsules:** W (and Ta) in liquid U and U–Pu at 2,100 °C for 1,000 h, isothermal and with a gradient (shared with [Q-020](../open-questions.md) and [Q-021](../open-questions.md)).
3. **Receiver panel:** AlN tube with flowing Al inside, facing a 2,000 °C radiator across 1 bar N2, at 0.8 MW/m² and a 1,550 °C surface. Measures recession and Al loss.
4. **Radiating-row mock-up:** electrically heated tube row to a cooled panel, checking isothermality and stirring in a liquid-metal-filled tube.
5. **Heterogeneous Monte Carlo** of a tube row and panel cell, then a 3D core with blankets: critical Pu, BR, Al-void and gap-flooding worths.
