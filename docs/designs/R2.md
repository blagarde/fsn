# R2: Radiatively coupled liquid U–Pu, lead-cooled Mo panels

Variant of [R1](R1.md) with the cooled wall changed to lead flowing in molybdenum panels. Fuel tubes, gap and core layout are those of R1. The point is chemistry: no ceramic anywhere in the heat path, and every pair is a refractory metal against one liquid or against vacuum. Lead–molybdenum is a known low-solubility pair, though only measured far below these temperatures. The costs are a lower outlet temperature, set by lead's boiling point, and a second emissive coating, because bare molybdenum absorbs poorly. Verdict: as plausible as R1, with simpler chemistry and ~100 K less outlet.

Numbers: *(calc)* from [analysis/new-geometries/](../../analysis/new-geometries/README.md), *(db)* from the property database, *(approx.)* otherwise. Comparison with R1 and A2: [scoping study](../studies/new-geometries-scoping.md#cooled-wall-side).

## Radiative heat-transfer sizing

With a coated tube (ε 0.8) at 2,000 °C, the average panel flux is 0.76 MW/m² at a 1,500 °C outlet, essentially R1's. The lead film and Mo wall add only 5 × 10⁻⁵ m²K/W *(calc)*. The receiver emissivity decides it: 0.27 MW/m² for bare Mo (ε 0.25), 0.51 for ε 0.5, 0.76 for ε 0.8 and 0.84 for ε 0.9 *(calc)*. Without a durable receiver coating the core roughly triples.

**Outlet limit.** For a 300 K boiling margin (taxonomy rule 1), lead at 1,400 °C needs 0.8 bar, at 1,500 °C 1.3 bar and at 1,700 °C 3.3 bar *(db, JANAF; above 1,746 °C extrapolated)*. A 1,500 °C outlet at ~1.3 bar is the reference.

## Neutronics

Infinite medium, 2,100 K *(calc, neutronics.py)*:

| Quantity | R2 | R1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pu/(U+Pu) for k∞ = 1.15 | 10.0% | 10.2% |
| Conversion ratio at k∞ = 1.15 | 1.24 | 1.32 |
| Median flux energy | ~215 keV | ~196 keV |
| Coolant fully voided | +1,300 ± 70 pcm | +3,000 ± 80 pcm |

Lead hardens the spectrum and its voiding worth is less than half of aluminium's. Molybdenum panels absorb more than AlN, which costs ~0.1 of conversion ratio. Pb-206/207 enrichment and thinner Mo walls would recover part of it. The gap is vacuum or argon, so the gap-gas worth is negligible. Flooding the gap with lead after a panel breach is worth −2,950 ± 33 pcm (computed in R1's composition, *calc, l7_detail.py*), a negative-reactivity event.

## Power cycle

Lead at 1,500 °C goes to a Pb-to-helium exchanger and a helium Brayton or combined cycle, as for [X1](X1.md). Lead's low heat capacity means a large inventory and pumping power (~20× aluminium per unit heat, see [aluminium coolant](../studies/aluminium-coolant.md)), so electromagnetic pumps do not work as well as with aluminium.
