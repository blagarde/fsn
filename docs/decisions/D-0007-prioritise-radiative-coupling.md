# D-0007: Prioritise the radiatively coupled pool (L7) for detailed design

- **Date:** 2026-11-20
- **Status:** accepted
- **Affects:** [geometry study](../studies/geometry-by-phase.md), new design family R, [A2](../designs/A2.md), [Q-006](../open-questions.md)

## Context

A2's critical risk is a tube wall that must face liquid U–Pu fuel on one side and liquid aluminium on the other at ~2,000 °C. The geometry study found that radiative coupling (geometry L7, heat-transport mode K-R) is the only arrangement in which no material touches both fuel and coolant, at the price of lower heat flux (about 0.3–0.8 MW/m² at 2,000 °C, against 3 MW/m² through A2's wall).

## Decision

The radiatively coupled pool becomes a design family with IDs R1, R2, … and takes priority for detailed design over the other new geometry candidates.

## Consequences

- The new-geometries analysis finishes L7 first, defines a reference design R1 and any justified variants, then scopes the remaining candidates.
- The other Q-006 candidates (printed lattice, fluidised bed, liquid-fuel pins, falling film, particle-cooled core) remain open until their scoping is reviewed.
- Key risks to resolve early: surface area needed and whether the larger core still breeds; container emissivity and its stability; gap atmosphere (vacuum or inert gas) and fission-product deposition on the cold wall.
